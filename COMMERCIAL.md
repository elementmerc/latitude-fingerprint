# Commercial Licensing

latitude-fingerprint is published under the
[AGPL-3.0-or-later](LICENSE). The AGPL is a strong copyleft
licence: anyone who modifies latitude-fingerprint, or runs a modified
version as a network-accessible service, must release the complete
corresponding source code of their version under the same terms.

For most users this works exactly the way it should: individuals,
researchers, security teams, journalists, schools, NGOs, and any
project willing to release its own source code can use latitude-fingerprint
freely under the AGPL with no further obligation.

For users who cannot meet AGPL's source-release terms (typically companies
integrating latitude-fingerprint into proprietary products, internal tools or
hosted services that they do not intend to open-source), a **commercial licence
is available** as an alternative.

## How dual licensing works

Every line of latitude-fingerprint is available under one of two terms:

| You can use latitude-fingerprint under | If you agree to |
|---|---|
| **AGPL-3.0-or-later** (the default) | Release your full corresponding source code, under the same licence, when you distribute or run your modified version as a service |
| **A paid commercial licence** (this document) | Use latitude-fingerprint inside proprietary or closed-source software without the source-release obligation |

You pick the licence that fits your situation. The codebase is the same
under either.

## Who typically needs the commercial licence

You probably do not need the commercial licence if you are:

- An individual using latitude-fingerprint as published
- A research group, journalism organisation, NGO, or educational
  institution
- An open-source project willing to license your derivative work under
  AGPL-3.0-or-later

You probably **do** need the commercial licence if you are:

- A corporation embedding latitude-fingerprint in a product you do not plan
  to open-source
- A company building an internal tool on top of latitude-fingerprint that
  you also do not plan to open-source
- A SaaS provider exposing latitude-fingerprint-derived functionality
  without releasing the surrounding service code under AGPL
- An OEM, reseller, integrator, or consultancy redistributing a modified
  latitude-fingerprint to clients under a proprietary or closed-source
  arrangement

If you are unsure which side of the line your use case falls on, ask.
We would rather have a five-minute conversation than have you guess
wrong.
